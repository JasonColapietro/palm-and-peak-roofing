Local SEO for contractors in West Palm Beach and Jupiter: service areas without fake addresses

How a Palm Beach County contractor can show up in local and AI search for the towns it serves, using only facts it can verify.

By Jason Colapietro, Suede AI · November 14, 2026 · West Palm Beach, Florida

The quickest way for a contractor to lose local visibility is to invent an office. Google's Business Profile guidelines do not allow P.O. boxes or unstaffed virtual offices, profiles that break the rule risk suspension, and the cleanup takes far longer than the shortcut saved. Roofers in West Palm Beach and Jupiter do not need a fake address to rank in the towns they serve. They need a consistent, verifiable record and pages that say something true about each place.

Service-area businesses are the normal case

Roofers work at the customer's home, not at a storefront. Google treats that as a service-area business: you can hide your address on your Business Profile and list the areas you serve instead. For a Palm Beach County roofer, that list might be West Palm Beach, Palm Beach Gardens, Jupiter, Juno Beach, Tequesta and Boca Raton. Keep it to places your crews genuinely reach. A list that stretches to Orlando tells Google and homeowners that you are guessing.

How the Palm and Peak concept handles location

Palm and Peak Roofing is a fictional concept that Suede AI built as a portfolio piece, so it has no real office to list. Instead of inventing one, the home page names the communities it is written for, Palm Beach, West Palm Beach and Palm Beach Gardens, as plain text, and the footer states that no services are offered. There is no LocalBusiness or RoofingContractor schema, because publishing a business record with made-up details would be the very mistake this note warns against.

A real contractor should go further: add structured data for the business with its legal name, verified phone number, license number and an areaServed list that matches the Business Profile. If the address is hidden on Google, leave it out of the site too.

Build town pages that earn their place

The common failure is the cloned city page: "Roof repair in Jupiter" and "Roof repair in Tequesta" with only the town name swapped. Those pages rarely rank for long, and AI answer engines have no reason to cite them. A town page earns its place when it contains something that is only true there:

  • Jupiter and Tequesta: homes close to the inlet and the Loxahatchee River face salt air, so fastener and flashing choices deserve their own explanation.
  • West Palm Beach: the city has designated historic districts, and exterior changes there may need extra review. Explain how you handle that conversation, and point homeowners to the city for the rules.
  • Palm Beach Gardens: many communities have architectural review for roof material and color. Describe how you prepare submissions with homeowners.

Add photos of your real projects in that town, with the homeowner's permission, and the questions people there actually ask. If you have nothing specific to say about a town yet, do not publish the page. Suede gives clients the same advice in every audit, even when it means building fewer pages. One honest page for West Palm Beach beats ten templated ones.

Consistency is what AI answer engines check

Generative engines build a picture of a business by comparing sources. If your website, Business Profile, state license record and directory listings disagree on your name, service area or phone number, the model hedges or leaves you out. Florida contractor licenses can be looked up on the state's licensing portal, so make sure the name there matches the name everywhere else.

Suede AI runs its own brand the same way. Jason Colapietro keeps one canonical founder record, his founder page, and every other site points to it instead of restating details that could drift. A checklist you can finish this week

This is the order Suede works through on a contractor audit:

  1. Open your Business Profile and confirm the service area lists only towns you serve.
  2. Make the business name identical on your site, profile, license record and top directories.
  3. Remove any address you cannot staff, from every listing.
  4. Pick one town where you have real projects and write a page with details only true there.
  5. Add a short FAQ to each service page in the homeowner's own words.
  6. Ask recent customers for reviews on platforms they already use. Never write them yourself or pay for them.
