Designing a roofing contractor website concept for Palm Beach County

A case study from Suede AI on the decisions behind Palm and Peak Roofing, a four-page contractor site concept designed in West Palm Beach.

By Jason Colapietro, Suede AI · November 14, 2026 · West Palm Beach, Florida

A roofing website has about ten seconds to answer two questions for a homeowner standing under a fresh ceiling stain: can these people help with my roof, and do they work where I live? Palm and Peak Roofing is a concept Suede AI built to answer both questions cleanly, and this note walks through each decision so contractors in West Palm Beach, Jupiter and the rest of Palm Beach County can borrow what works.

About this site: Palm & Peak Roofing is a fictional company and a design concept built by Suede AI. No roofing services are offered here, and the concept deliberately lists no license numbers, reviews, phone numbers or street address.

Getting code accepted by strangers teaches you to make small, readable changes that hold up to review. A four-page contractor site deserves the same discipline.

The page structure: one home page, three service pages

The concept has a home page and three service pages: residential roofing, roof inspections and storm restoration. Each service page follows the same pattern:

  1. A short hero with a breadcrumb and a one-sentence lede that says what the page covers.
  2. A list of three focus areas, such as roof covering, edges and roof details, and drainage.
  3. A three-step process written in plain language.
  4. Three questions homeowners actually ask, answered in two or three sentences each.
  5. Links to the other two services and back home.

Repeating the pattern helps people scan, because once they learn the layout on one page they know where to look on the next. It also helps answer engines. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI results tend to quote short, self-contained passages, and an FAQ answer that stands on its own is easy to lift accurately.

Copy that does not promise what it cannot prove

Roofing sites are full of claims nobody can check: "best in Palm Beach", "five-star service", review counts with no source. The concept avoids all of them. The inspection page says plainly that access and visibility vary by property and that limitations should be explained. The storm page tells homeowners to share photos taken from a safe spot and not to climb onto a roof.

That restraint is a ranking strategy as much as an ethical one. AI search layers cross-check what a business says against what others say about it, and invented praise invites contradiction. Suede practices this on its own brand: when Google's Web Guide answered a "Suede labs ai review" query by calling Suede Labs AI "highly rated by users, earning a 5/5 rating from verified reviews", the team published the dated capture of what Google said instead of turning it into a homepage boast. Third-party words carry weight because they are not yours.

Speed and accessibility as trust signals

The concept is static HTML and one shared stylesheet, served by GitHub Pages with no build step. The only large asset is a single locally hosted WebP hero image of roughly 340 KB, with its width and height declared so the layout does not jump while it loads. Navigation targets are at least 44 pixels tall, there is a skip link, focus outlines are visible, and motion is turned off for people who ask for reduced motion.

None of that shows up in a screenshot, but it shows up on a phone with one bar of signal after a summer storm in Jupiter. A page that renders in a second earns more patience than one with a carousel and a chat widget fighting for bandwidth. That is the Suede default for every contractor build.

Geo signals without inventing a business

The home page closes with "At home in Palm Beach County" and names Palm Beach, West Palm Beach and Palm Beach Gardens as plain text. There is no LocalBusiness schema with a made-up address, no fake phone number and no hours. A real contractor would add verified details from its license and its Google Business Profile, and the companion note on local SEO without fake addresses covers how to do that properly.

These notes do carry location context, but only as article metadata that says which places the writing is about. That is the honest version of geo SEO: describe where you work and what you know about it, and let the verified business record do the rest.
